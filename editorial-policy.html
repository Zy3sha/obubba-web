Editorial policy: how we write and check our guides

How the OBubba team writes and checks its baby sleep, feeding and pregnancy guides: the sources we use, how AI tools are used, what we do not claim, and how to report a mistake.

This page explains how the guides, calculators and comparisons on obubba.com are made, so you can decide how much weight to give them. It applies to every page on this site.

Who writes our content

Our content is published by the OBubba team at OLife Labs Limited, the UK company that makes the OBubba app. We sign our pages as "The OBubba team". We do not invent authors, personas or expert profiles.

How pages are written

We use AI writing tools to help research and draft pages, and to help translate them. The OBubba team decides what we publish, and we build each page on public sources that you can open yourself. We link to those sources on the page wherever we make a health-related statement.

Before a page goes live, our site build runs automated checks, for example for claims about OBubba that we have ruled out and for missing images. These checks catch some problems; they are not a substitute for professional advice.

Who checks them

We are a small team, and we do not yet have a qualified health professional reviewing our pages. Until we do, we stay close to official guidance rather than offering our own opinions on health matters. If that changes, we will name the reviewer here and on every page they have checked.

The sources we use

For UK families we follow UK guidance first:

  • the NHS, including NHS Best Start in Life
  • The Lullaby Trust for safer sleep
  • NICE guidance
  • BASIS, the Baby Sleep Information Source at Durham University

We also use the World Health Organization (including the WHO Child Growth Standards), the American Academy of Pediatrics and peer-reviewed research. Linking to an organisation means its material informs the page. It does not mean that organisation has reviewed or endorsed OBubba.

When a number has no official source, we say so. For example, no health body publishes official wake-window figures, so our wake-window tables are described as typical ranges, not rules. The ranges on our calculators and sleep guides come from the same tables the OBubba app uses, so the site and the app do not contradict each other.

What we will not do

  • We do not give medical diagnoses or make medical claims. OBubba is not a medical device.
  • We do not use alarming language to worry parents into action.
  • We do not invent statistics. A number on our site comes from a named source or from OBubba data with the method explained.
  • We do not post as parents in forums or publish fake reviews.

Data from the OBubba app

Where we share findings from OBubba logs, they are aggregated and anonymised, and we explain how they were worked out and what they cannot tell you. We never publish anything that could identify a family or a baby.

Comparisons with other apps

Our comparison pages use each app's own store listing or website, with the date we checked. A comparison table only lists features that every app in it says it has on its own pages. We never mark a feature as missing from another app. If you work for one of these apps and something is out of date, please tell us and we will correct it.

Translations

Some pages are available in Spanish, French, German, Portuguese and Italian. They are machine translated from English, and safety details such as ages and allergens are always taken from the English version. Our guidance follows UK sources, so please also check the advice from the health service where you live.

Updates

Each guide shows the date it was published. When we make a meaningful change, such as new sources, corrected facts or updated guidance, we update the page and its date. We review pages when official guidance changes and whenever someone reports a problem.

Report a mistake

If you spot something wrong, out of date or unclear, please email hello@obubba.com with the page link and what you think needs changing. We read every report, and when we get something wrong we correct it.

Not medical advice

Everything on this site is general information. It is not a substitute for advice from your health visitor, midwife, GP or pharmacist. If you are worried about your baby, trust your instincts and get help: call NHS 111, or 999 in an emergency. Outside the UK, contact your local health service.
