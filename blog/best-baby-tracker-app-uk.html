Best baby tracking apps UK: the short answer

For a UK family, the best baby tracking app is the one that records the few details you genuinely need, keeps both parents or carers on the same current picture, and makes it easy to explain the day without turning care into homework.

OBubba is a relevant option if you want pregnancy-to-baby continuity, feeds, nappies, sleep, development, reports and shared care in one app. A simpler single-purpose timer may suit you better if you only want to record one category and do not need family sharing or broader guidance.

This comparison is published by OBubba, so it is not an independent ranking. It uses the features and boundaries described in each app's current UK App Store listing on 6 September 2026. Store features, prices and terms can change, so check the live listing before choosing.

The best baby tracker app should make the day easier, not busier

New parents in the UK are not looking for a spreadsheet with prettier buttons. They are trying to answer tiny urgent questions while tired: when did baby last feed, was that nappy wet or dirty, how long was the nap, what happened before the unsettled evening, and what do we need to tell the health visitor?

The best baby tracker app is the one that reduces the amount held in one parent's head.

OBubba is built around that idea. It keeps feeds, breastfeeding, bottles, sleep, naps, nappies, weaning, medicine notes, growth, milestones and family handovers together, so the baby day is easier to understand.

What UK parents should look for

  • Nappy tracking, not just diaper wording.
  • Feed tracking for breastfeeding, bottles, pumping and mixed feeding.
  • Sleep and nap tracking that keeps safe sleep context separate from guesses.
  • Wake window support that respects baby cues.
  • Reports or summaries for health visitor, GP, nursery or family conversations.
  • Partner and carer sharing so the care day does not live in one exhausted memory.
  • Clear privacy and no strange data selling.

For a shorter comparison, work through the baby tracker app checklist using the moments your family finds hardest to remember.

Where OBubba is different

OBubba uses the language parents actually use: nappies, carers, health visitors, routines and handovers. Its clearest difference is not simply the number of things it tracks. It starts during pregnancy, continues on the same journey after birth, and separates ongoing parent access from a temporary no-login handover for another trusted carer.

That may fit a family that wants one wider record. It may be more than you need if you only want a basic feed or nappy timer. A useful app should remove more work than it creates.

Evidence-aware, not bossy

Baby tracking should sit alongside trusted guidance, not pretend to replace it. Parents should follow their midwife, health visitor, GP or paediatrician for medical concerns.

For general context, UK parents can also use official sources such as NHS safer sleep advice and NHS responsive bottle feeding advice.

When OBubba may not be the right fit

Choose a simpler tool if you only need one isolated timer, do not want to keep a wider baby record or know that extra categories will add pressure. Choose OBubba when one connected record, gradual personalised guidance and sharing between the people caring for the baby would remove more work than the logging creates.
