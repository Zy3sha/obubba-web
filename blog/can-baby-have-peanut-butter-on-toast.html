Can My Baby Have Peanut Butter on Toast?

Yes, once your baby is ready for solids. Here is how to serve peanut butter on toast safely, handle two new allergens, and know when to ask for help.

The toast is on your plate, the peanut butter is open, and suddenly breakfast feels like a big decision. Can your baby have some too?

Yes. Once your baby is ready for solids, usually around six months, the NHS says peanut butter can be spread on a food such as toast. Never offer a spoonful of peanut butter by itself or whole peanuts: both can be choking hazards. If peanut and wheat are new to your baby, start with peanut on a food they already eat instead, so you can tell which new food a reaction followed. NHS food-safety advice and NHS allergy guidance explain the two separate questions: safe texture and new allergens.

Make the toast a baby-sized food, not a sticky mouthful

Choose smooth peanut butter and spread it across the toast rather than offering it straight from the spoon. The NHS specifically recommends nut butter as a spread or mixed into another food, such as porridge. A thick lump on a spoon is not the same as a spread on food.

Lightly toast white bread, or use brown bread; soft white bread can form a doughy ball if it is not chewed well. Cut the bread into narrow strips that your baby can manage. Sit your baby safely upright, stay with them throughout the meal and let them go at their own pace. These are the NHS food-preparation steps, not a guarantee that choking cannot happen.

If toast is too tricky for your baby today, you do not need to force the finger food. Smooth peanut butter mixed well into a familiar porridge or purée is another NHS-supported way to serve it. Let the texture match what your baby can already manage.

Is it a first peanut taste, a first wheat taste, or both?

Bread commonly contains wheat. If your baby has already eaten wheat without a problem, the peanut butter is the one new allergenic food at this meal. If wheat is also new, choose a familiar base for the peanut first, or offer wheat separately on another occasion. The NHS recommends introducing foods that can trigger allergy one at a time and in very small amounts so a possible reaction is easier to spot. Check the bread and spread labels for other ingredients too.

There is no need to turn one breakfast into an allergen challenge. Pick a time when you can stay nearby and observe your baby. Offer a small amount, without pressure to finish it. If your baby turns away, breakfast can end there. If peanut is tolerated, keep it in your baby's usual varied diet, rather than treating that first taste as a one-off test. The NHS advises continuing tolerated allergenic foods as part of the usual diet.

If your baby has eczema, a diagnosed food allergy, or there is a family history of food allergy, eczema, asthma or hay fever, ask your GP or health visitor before introducing peanut. They can advise on your baby's situation. For a baby born prematurely, ask your health visitor or GP when to begin solids. Readiness matters more than copying someone else's breakfast timetable; the NHS lists the signs of readiness.

What if something changes after breakfast?

Food-allergy symptoms often appear within minutes but may take up to two hours. A raised itchy rash, swelling of the lips or face, vomiting, coughing or wheezing can all matter. If you suspect a reaction, stop offering that food and seek advice from your GP or NHS 111 before trying it again; jot down what was eaten and when symptoms began. A note helps a clinician, but it cannot diagnose an allergy. NHS guidance lists symptoms and when to seek help.

Call 999 immediately if your baby is struggling to breathe, has sudden swelling of the tongue or throat, becomes pale or blue, or is limp or not responding normally. Those can be signs of a severe allergic reaction; NHS emergency guidance sets out the warning signs. If your baby is choking, follow NHS choking first aid and call 999 when needed. Choking and allergy are different emergencies.

Keep a tiny record, then get on with the day

If your baby ate peanut butter on toast, a useful note is simply: smooth peanut butter on lightly toasted wheat bread; time; roughly how much was eaten; any response.
