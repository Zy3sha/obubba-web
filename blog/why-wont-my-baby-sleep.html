Why Won't My Baby Sleep? Common Context to Review

A calm guide to reviewing feeds, comfort, recent sleep and routine changes without pretending a log can diagnose the cause.

It's late, you've tried everything, and your baby is still wide awake (or wide awake again). When you're this tired, "why won't my baby sleep?" stops being a question and starts being a plea. A log may help you review what changed, but it cannot establish the cause or promise a fix.

Here are common possibilities to consider. A page or tracker cannot tell you which one applies tonight.

The usual suspects

  • A long awake stretch. Some babies become harder to settle after staying awake longer than usual. Fussiness or a short nap can be useful context, but neither proves the cause.
  • Undertired. The opposite — put down before enough sleep pressure has built. Tell-tale sign: happy, chatty, "playing" in the cot, or a bedtime that drags on for an hour.
  • Timing may have shifted. Recent awake time can be one clue among feeds, comfort, illness, development and the sleep environment. See wake windows by age.
  • Hunger. A growth spurt or a too-small last feed means they genuinely can't settle. Cluster feeding in the evening is normal in the early months.
  • A quick wake after bedtime. Some families call this a false start. Timing may be one possible context, but a short first sleep does not identify the cause by itself.
  • Developmental change. Sleep patterns often change around 4 months and at other stages, but age alone does not explain a particular night.
  • Discomfort. Teething, a cold, trapped wind, or silent reflux — see colic and reflux support.
  • The sleep environment. Too bright, too warm, too stimulating, or too quiet after the womb's constant whoosh.
  • A familiar settling pattern. Feeding, rocking or holding may be part of how a baby settles. That can be useful context without making it a problem that must be removed.

How to work out which one it is

Play detective with a few quick questions:

  • When did they last sleep, and for how long? Long gap or skipped nap → likely overtired. Barely any awake time → undertired.
  • When did they last feed, and how much? Long gap or a snacky feed → check hunger.
  • Did they go down, then wake quickly? Record the timing and what happened around it, then review the pattern across several nights rather than assigning one cause.
  • Anything new this week? New skill, tooth, cold, travel, or roughly the right age → suspect a regression/leap.
  • Are they waking happy or upset? Happy at 5am with hours of day sleep behind them → often too much day sleep or an early bedtime. Upset and frantic → tiredness or discomfort.

Sometimes timing is relevant; sometimes it is not. Review a few days rather than treating one rough night as proof. If tracking itself is becoming stressful, use this wake-window reset without catch-up logging.

The hard part: it changes constantly

The frustrating truth is that the reason changes as your baby grows. What worked last month stops working; the "right" wake window drifts; a leap arrives uninvited. Reading it correctly, night after night, on no sleep, is genuinely hard.
