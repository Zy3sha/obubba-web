Gentle Sleep Training Methods Compared: No Cry It Out

Gentle, responsive ways to help your baby fall asleep with less help, compared by age: routines, hands-on settling, gradual steps and staying close.

Last checked 18 November 2026. You can help a baby fall asleep with less help without leaving them to cry. The gentlest changes come first: a steady wake time, a short calm bedtime routine and a bedtime that fits the day. Many families need nothing more. If you want to go further, gentle methods let you do a little less each week while staying close. A bit of grizzling while settling is normal. If your baby is crying, go to them and comfort them, every time.

Start here: the day before the method

Before changing how your baby falls asleep, check the day around it. Gentle changes here often do the most:

  • A steady morning wake, within about half an hour each day.
  • A short, predictable bedtime routine, about 20 to 30 minutes, the same steps in the same order. In one large study, a simple routine on its own helped babies fall asleep faster and wake less within three weeks.
  • A bedtime that fits the day. A bedtime that's too late often makes nights shorter: see what we see in OBubba families. If bedtime is very late, bring it earlier gradually.
  • Naps that leave room for the night. A long or late last nap can push bedtime later.

The one rule in every method

> Fussing: stay close and give them a moment. Your voice or a hand is often enough. > Crying: go to them and comfort them, every time. Pick them up if they need it. There is no clock. > You know your baby's cry better than any app. If you're not sure, go to them.

By age

  • Under 4 months: focus on the day, the routine, feeding and safe sleep. Settle your baby however works: in arms, with a feed, with touch. Babies this young have no regular sleep cycles yet, and sleep training shows no benefit under 6 months.
  • 4 to 6 months: a routine, a bedtime that fits, and hands-on settling or a first gentle prop swap if you want one.
  • 6 to 12 months: any of the methods above. Staying close and the chair suit babies who are more aware when you leave.
  • 12 months and up: the same gentle steps, plus words and simple choices ("this book or that one?").

How long does it take?

Usually one to three weeks, judged over the week rather than night by night. Ill, teething, travelling or a big change? Pause and come back to it. Nothing is lost.

Frequently asked questions

Can you sleep train without crying?

Yes. Gentle methods help your baby need a little less help over time while you stay close. Some grizzling while they settle is normal. If they cry, go to them.

Is a little crying OK?

Brief fussing or grizzling while a baby settles is normal, and you can stay close and give them a moment. If it builds into crying, go to them and comfort them.

What about controlled crying or timed checks?

This guide only covers methods that keep you responding to your baby. We don't explain methods that ask you to leave your baby to cry for set times.

Which method is best?

The one that suits your baby and your family. Many families only need the routine and bedtime fixes. For a baby who needs more help, the gradual prop swap or staying close are gentle places to start.

When should I talk to someone?

If your baby's sleep worries you, or you're exhausted, speak to your health visitor, GP or pediatrician. If you're struggling with your own mood, tell them too. You matter.
