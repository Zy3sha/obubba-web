Sending Expressed Breast Milk to Nursery: A Calm Packing Checklist

What to label, how to keep expressed milk cold, and what to tell nursery so the first handover is clear and safe.

sending expressed breast milk to nurserybreast milk nursery storageexpressed milk handover

The milk is ready. The nursery bag is not. Do you send one big bottle, three little ones, frozen milk, or a note that says “please just see how it goes”?

Start with the nursery's own milk policy, then make each portion easy to identify and keep it cold on the journey. Agree where staff will put it when you arrive, how they will offer it, and what they will tell you your baby actually drank. You do not need to predict a perfect amount before the first day.

This is for expressed breast milk, not prepared infant formula; the NHS gives different transport limits for formula. If your baby was premature or is medically vulnerable, follow the storage and feeding plan from their clinical team.

Ask nursery four questions before packing

  1. What labels do you need? Many settings ask for your child's name, the expression date and the amount on each container. Ask about their exact format and whether they accept storage bags, bottles or frozen milk. The NHS advises labelling and dating stored breast milk; your nursery may need more details to prevent mix-ups.
  2. Where will milk go at drop-off? Find out who takes it straight to a fridge and how they check storage temperature. Do not leave a cool bag in a hallway and assume it has been put away.
  3. How will they offer it? Tell staff about your baby's usual cues and any feeding plan. Ask whether they can offer small portions and note what was drunk, not just what was poured or offered.
  4. What happens to unused milk? Agree how they handle an unopened portion, a warmed portion and a bottle your baby has drunk from. These are not the same situation.

The night before: label small, sealed portions

Use a sterilised container or a breast-milk storage bag. Label each portion clearly and check the lid or seal before it goes in the bag. Smaller quantities can reduce waste while you learn what your baby usually takes away from you; the NHS recommends storing milk in small quantities. You can ask nursery how much of a backup portion they would like rather than sending your whole supply.

Put chilled milk at the back of the fridge, not in the door. The NHS says it can be kept there for up to eight days at 4°C or below; if you are unsure of your fridge temperature, or it runs between 5°C and 10°C, use it within three days. Your nursery may apply a shorter policy, so use the stricter limit. Do not rely on smell, a phone log or a handwritten guess to decide that milk is safe.

If using frozen milk, agree with nursery whether they want it frozen or already thawed. The NHS recommends thawing in the fridge when possible, using defrosted milk straight away and never re-freezing it. Do not microwave milk to hurry the morning along; it can create hot spots.

At drop-off: cold bag, direct handover

Move milk from the fridge to an insulated cool bag with ice packs just before you leave. The NHS says breast milk that was first cooled in the fridge can travel in a cool bag with ice packs for up to 24 hours. That is a transport limit under those conditions, not an invitation to leave it warm or sit in a car all day. Hand it directly to the staff member who will store it, and follow the nursery's handling rules.

A useful note is short: “Milk for Amira: two sealed chilled portions, expressed yesterday; please refrigerate on arrival. Offer when she shows hunger cues; tell us how much she drinks and whether any unopened portion remains.” Add any individual clinical instructions. Keep names, dates and amounts on the actual containers too—the note can get separated from the bag.

The NHS advises responsive bottle feeding: watch your baby and do not force them to finish a bottle. If they have drunk from a bottle of breast milk, the NHS says to use it within one hour and throw away what is left. A nursery log should therefore record the milk consumed, not treat a half-finished bottle as the next feed.

If your baby takes less than expected on a nursery day, look at the whole day—including feeds with you, wet nappies and how your baby seems—rather than trying to make one bottle hit a target. Speak to your health visitor, infant-feeding team or GP if intake worries you, nappies are fewer than usual, or your baby seems unwell. If your baby is hard to wake or struggling to breathe, follow the NHS urgent-help guidance and call 999 for an emergency.
