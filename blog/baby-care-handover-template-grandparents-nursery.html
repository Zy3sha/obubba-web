The short answer

A useful baby handover tells the next carer what happened recently, what is still unresolved and where they should look for professional instructions. It does not need to become a complete diary.

Start with the last feed, the last nappy when you are watching output, recent sleep context, medicine details only when relevant, and one open question. If your baby seems unwell or you are worried, contact an appropriate health professional instead of relying on a handover note.

What each line is for

Last feed

Record the time and the detail the next carer actually needs, such as breast side, bottle amount or food offered. Do not turn an ordinary handover into a target your baby must meet.

Last nappy

Include this when your family is watching wet or dirty nappies, or when a professional has asked you to keep a record. Otherwise it can be left out.

Recent sleep

The last sleep and wake time can give the next carer useful context. It is a record of what happened, not a promise about when the next sleep will happen.

Medicine

Only include medicine when it is relevant. Copy the medicine name, amount and time exactly from the instructions already given to your family. A family handover is not a dosing guide; if anything is unclear, check the label or contact the appropriate professional.

One open question

Finish with the thing the next carer still needs to notice or decide. Examples might be whether a bottle was accepted, whether a planned call has been made, or whether a familiar settling method helped.

What not to record by default

You do not need to record every mood, sound or minute simply because a field exists. A shorter record can be easier to keep current and easier for another tired person to scan.

Pause or simplify tracking if it is creating more anxiety than clarity. Keep the information a health professional has specifically asked you to record, and ask them what level of detail is useful when you are unsure.

Keep the handover useful

Treat this checklist as a starting point, not a rule. Keep the lines that prevent repeated questions in your family, remove the rest, and update the open question when care changes hands.
