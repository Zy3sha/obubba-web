Waking Up Drenched After Birth? A Calm Check for Night Sweats

Night sweats can happen in the weeks after birth. Here is what may help tonight, which symptoms need a call and what to tell your midwife or GP.

You wake for a feed and realise your pyjamas are soaked, even though the room is not hot. Sweating at night can happen in the weeks after birth, but you do not have to guess that every drenching night is “just hormones”. If you also have a fever, shivers, tummy pain or feel unwell, contact your midwife, maternity unit or GP promptly. If the sweating keeps happening or worries you, ask your GP about it.

An NHS hospital's postnatal guidance describes sweating after birth as often worse at night and says it can last several weeks, sometimes longer while breastfeeding. It also says to contact your GP if you have a fever or it is not settling. That is general guidance, not a way to identify the cause of your own symptoms.

A small plan for tonight

If you feel otherwise well, you can make the next wake-up less miserable without turning it into another job:

  • Put a dry top and towel or spare sheet within reach before bed.
  • Drink when you are thirsty and keep water nearby, particularly if you are feeding your baby overnight.
  • Try light cotton clothing and a comfortable room temperature. A lukewarm shower before bed may help; these are simple comfort ideas in the NHS postnatal leaflet, not a treatment for a medical cause.
  • If you feel hot or shivery, check your temperature if you can. Note any other symptoms rather than relying on the wet sheets alone.

You do not need to keep a perfect diary. If you call, it is useful to say when the sweating started, whether you have measured a temperature, whether it is improving, and whether you have pain, shivers, unusual discharge or breast symptoms. A partner can help by taking the next nappy change or bringing dry clothes; the point is to get you cared for too.

When should you call?

Seek medical advice promptly if you have fever or shivering, tummy pain, or vaginal discharge that looks or smells unusual. Cambridge University Hospitals' postnatal advice lists these as reasons for urgent assessment, and also flags breast pain, redness or flu-like symptoms that may need advice. If you are still under maternity care, use the contact number your team gave you; otherwise contact your GP or NHS 111. Do not wait for a routine postnatal check if you feel unwell.

Get emergency help for chest pain or breathlessness, sudden very heavy bleeding (especially with dizziness), or other severe symptoms. The NHS post-birth guidance and Cambridge University Hospitals flag these as urgent. Call 999 if you need an ambulance.

If there is no other symptom but your clothes and bedding are regularly soaked, the NHS night-sweats guidance says to see a GP when it keeps waking you or worries you. It is okay to ask earlier; you do not have to prove that you are ill before making a call.
