What matters most for baby sleep

Two things move the needle on infant sleep, and every good sleep app should support both:

  1. The right naps. Naps that are too early, too late, too short or too many are the most common reason a baby fights bedtime or wakes overnight. Nap timing built from your baby's own logs, not a generic age chart, is the single most useful thing an app can give you.
  2. A steady bedtime routine. A consistent, age-appropriate bedtime that flexes with how the day actually went is what takes the guesswork out of the evening.

Judge any "sleep tracker" on whether it helps with those two things, not just how pretty the log looks.

The comparison

OBubba: Baby Sleep Tracker

Best for parents who want to understand why sleep went sideways and what to change. OBubba learns your baby's own rhythm and predicts the next nap, flags the wake window, and sets tonight's bedtime from your baby's own day. It works like a sleep consultant: Luna, the in-app sleep coach, explains night wakes and split nights in plain language, and the whole day (feeds, weaning, teething, milestones, growth) sits alongside sleep so you can see how the day shaped the night. Free to download with core tracking free; premium adds personalised nap and bedtime guidance and reports. Partner sync and carer handovers are included.

Huckleberry

A well-known, polished option with nap and bedtime predictions ("SweetSpot") and an optional paid sleep-consultation add-on. Strong brand and a large user base. Its deeper guidance sits behind a higher-priced premium tier, and the experience leans on its own model rather than a transparent, plain-language "why."

Napper

Beautifully designed and popular, with schedules, sounds and community content. Great if you want a visually calming, mainstream experience. It is more of a guided schedule and logging app than a diagnostic one, so it tells you the plan more than the reason behind it.

nappi

A newer all-in-one tracker with nap predictions and AI logging. Clean and improving quickly. Still early, with a smaller track record than the established names.

NapNap

A newer sleep-first tracker built around wake-window nap prediction, available in many languages. A focused, capable option if you mainly want next-nap timing.

Classic loggers (Baby Tracker and similar)

Reliable for recording feeds, sleep and nappies. If all you need is a tidy diary, they do the job, but they do not predict naps or coach a bedtime.

How to choose without wasting a month

Pick one app and log sleep for about a week so it can learn your baby. Then look for three things: does it predict the next nap in a way that matches reality, does it give you a bedtime built from your baby's real day, and can your partner or carer see the same picture. An app that gets those right will fix more nights than any feature list.

Free: turn on nap reminders and OBubba nudges you when it’s time to wind down. Premium: the next nap in advance and a countdown, free to try for 14 days, no card. Use the trial to test the nap prediction and bedtime guidance on your own baby before deciding.
