What Time Should Christmas Dinner Be With a Baby? Nap-Friendly Times by Age

The calmest time to serve Christmas dinner, open presents or set off for the in-laws, worked out around your baby's naps for each age from 3 months to 2 years.

Most UK Christmas dinners land somewhere between 1pm and 3pm. For a lot of babies, that is right in the middle of a nap.

You do not need to move Christmas for the baby, or the baby for Christmas. You need one good guess at when your baby will be awake and happy, and to send that time to whoever is cooking.

Quick answer: if your baby wakes around 7am, the easiest dinner times are about 1:30pm for a 6 month old, about 12pm or 4pm for an 8 to 15 month old on two naps, and about 3pm for a toddler on one midday nap. Under 6 months, eat when you can and let the baby nap on and off around you.

The times below come from the same age-based sleep tables that OBubba's free baby sleep schedule maker uses. They are a starting point, not a rule. Every baby is different, and your own baby's last few days tell you more than any table.

If your baby usually wakes earlier or later, move every time in the table by the same amount. A 6am waker on two naps is ready for an 11am lunch; an 8am waker on one nap suits a 4pm dinner.

Three ways to make the day work

1. Send the host one time, not a schedule

Hosts can work with a single time. "Could we eat at about 3? That's when she'll be awake and happy" is easier to plan around than a list of naps. If the dinner time is fixed, look at the table for the nap it clashes with and plan where that nap will happen.

2. Protect the first nap

Sleep consultants often say the first nap of the day matters most. A baby who had a good morning nap copes far better with a nap on the go later, or a bedtime that runs a little late. If you are travelling, do the morning nap at home and drive at the time of the next one.

3. Pick one thing that can slip

Christmas has more moments than a baby has awake time. Choose which one can move: presents can wait until after the nap, the walk can become a pram nap, and pudding can happen without the baby at the table. Bedtime can be a bit late on one night without undoing anything.

If a nap happens somewhere new

  • In the car: car seats keep babies safe while travelling, but the Lullaby Trust says they are not designed to be a main sleeping place. If your baby falls asleep in the car seat, take them out when you arrive and lay them on a firm, flat surface to sleep.
  • At someone else's house: a travel cot or pram with a flat lying position in a quieter room works for many babies. On the back, feet to foot, nothing loose near the face, and not too warm in a busy, heated room.
  • On you, on the sofa: lovely while you are awake. The Lullaby Trust's advice is never to fall asleep on a sofa or armchair with your baby. If you are drowsy after dinner, pass the baby to someone who is alert or lay them down.
  • After a drink: the Lullaby Trust says that if you have drunk any alcohol, your baby is safest in their own separate sleep space that night.

A late night is fine

If Christmas runs past bedtime, pack the pyjamas, do a short version of the usual wind-down wherever you are, and put the baby down when you get home. One late night is normal. Common questions

What time should Christmas dinner be with a baby?

It depends on your baby's naps. For a baby who wakes at 7am, about 1:30pm suits many 6 month olds, about 12pm or 4pm suits babies on two naps (about 8 to 15 months), and about 3pm suits toddlers on one midday nap.

Should I keep my baby's nap schedule on Christmas Day?

Keep the first nap if you can, and let the rest flex. A nap in the pram or on the move is fine for one day. Watch your baby's tired cues rather than the clock.

My baby will nap in the car on the way. Is that OK?

Sleeping on a car journey is common. The Lullaby Trust advises taking your baby out of the car seat when you arrive and laying them on a firm, flat surface, and taking regular breaks on long drives.

Will one late Christmas night ruin my baby's routine?

No. A single late night is normal. Go back to the usual wake-up time and routine the next day; many babies settle back within a day or two.
