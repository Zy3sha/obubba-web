How the schedule is built

Each awake stretch is taken from the age-typical wake window, starting short in the morning and getting longer towards bedtime. Nap lengths come from the typical day sleep for the age, and bedtime is kept inside a healthy window for the age, usually 6pm to 8pm from about three months.

Why a late bedtime can backfire

It sounds backwards, but a bedtime that drifts much past 8pm often leads to more night waking and earlier mornings, not a longer lie-in. An earlier bedtime after a day of short naps is a common, gentle fix.

When the day does not go to plan

Short naps, a busy day out or a nap in the car all happen. The next wake window is what matters. OBubba re-plans the rest of the day live from what actually happened, so one wobbly nap does not throw out the whole evening.

Questions parents ask

Frequently asked questions

What time should my baby go to bed?

For most babies from about three months, a bedtime somewhere between 6pm and 8pm works well. Newborns have no body clock yet, so a later, more flexible bedtime is normal at first.

How many naps should my baby have?

Roughly 4 to 6 short naps in the newborn weeks, 3 to 4 at three to five months, 3 at around six months, 2 from about eight months, and 1 from somewhere between 13 and 18 months.

Should I wake my baby from a nap?

Sometimes it helps to cap a very long late nap so bedtime is not pushed late. During the day, many parents protect bedtime by ending the last nap a full wake window before bed.

Does a schedule mean my baby has to follow the clock?

No. Think of it as a rhythm. Most babies do best on a predictable pattern with a little flexibility either way.
