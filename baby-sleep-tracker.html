A calmer four-step sleep log

You do not need to reconstruct yesterday. Keep the moments that help your family and leave out the ones that do not.

Step 1

Start the sleep that is happening

Use the live timer for a nap or night sleep. If the real start was earlier, correct the time rather than inventing a neat schedule.

Step 2

Stop it when the sleep ends

Save the real end time and record a night wake when it is useful. A missed tap can be corrected without backfilling the whole day.

Step 3

Keep nearby context together

Log the feed, nappy or care change that matters so the sleep record stays connected to the baby day.

Step 4

Use less when less feels better

Treat guidance as a prompt to check your baby. Pause, simplify or stop tracking if the record is creating more pressure than relief.

Logged patterns offer context, not commands

  • Suggested nap and bedtime times are flexible guides, not guarantees, medical advice or a reason to ignore feeding and tired cues.
  • Babies have individual sleep patterns, and those patterns can change with age, illness, travel, feeding and ordinary development.
  • An app cannot diagnose a sleep problem or promise longer sleep, fewer wakes or easier naps.
  • Timing guidance never replaces current safer-sleep advice. Follow official guidance for every sleep and seek individual professional help when sleep, feeding, breathing or wellbeing concerns you.
  • Tracking is optional. A useful record matters more than a complete one.

Current parent questions and product evidence

Parents describe two very different experiences: tracking can free memory and help couples coordinate, or it can create pressure when timings become expectations. Current NHS guidance also stresses that babies have individual and changing sleep patterns. This page is designed around those realities.

  • NHS: Helping your baby to sleep — The NHS explains that babies have their own waking and sleeping patterns, that those patterns change, and that individual advice is available from a health visitor.
  • NHS: Newborn and baby sleeping advice — The NHS brings safer sleep, individual sleep patterns and coping with tiredness together for parents.
  • The Lullaby Trust: Safer sleep overview — The Lullaby Trust provides current safer-sleep information that remains separate from any timing suggestion in an app.
  • Current parent discussion about useful tracking and pressure — Parents describe memory relief and easier teamwork, while others explain why they simplified or stopped when tracking increased anxiety.
  • Parent discussion about whether timing apps are essential — Parents describe sleep apps as useful for some families and unnecessary or too rigid for others.

An invited parent can use Partner Sync to contribute to the same live baby record. Bubba Care is the separate private browser handover for another trusted carer.

What if sleep tracking makes me anxious?

Use only the records that genuinely help. You can simplify, pause or stop tracking, and a useful partial record is better than a complete record that creates pressure.
