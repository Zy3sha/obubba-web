🐦 Pheasant for babies: when and how to serve it

Can babies eat pheasant? Yes, from about 6 months, in small occasional amounts. It is lean and iron-rich, but shot game can contain lead pellets and tiny bones, so careful preparation matters.

From 6 monthsTake care with shape and textureIron rich

Good source of: Protein, Iron, Vitamin B6, Selenium

How to serve pheasant by age

6 to 9 months

Cook pheasant thoroughly, ideally slow cooked or braised so it stays moist, and strip all meat from the bones. Shred and blend with vegetables or offer large soft strips. Braising it with stock and root vegetables keeps the lean meat moist.

9 to 12 months

Offer finely shredded pheasant in casseroles or with mashed root vegetables.

12 months and up

Serve small pieces of tender casseroled pheasant as part of family meals.

Choking safety

Pheasant has many small, sharp bones and can contain shot. Check every piece carefully by hand. Lean meat can be dry; serve it moist. Avoid giving drumsticks to babies, as they contain many thin, needle-like tendons and bones.

Gagging is common and noisy while babies learn to eat. Choking is usually silent. Always stay with your baby while they eat, sitting upright. How to help a choking baby (NHS).

Good to know

  • Game shot with lead can contain lead fragments; the FSA advises toddlers and children eat lead-shot game only rarely.
  • Remove any visible shot and the meat around it.

Easy ideas

  • Pheasant casserole with root veg
  • Pheasant and pearl barley stew

Questions parents ask

Can babies eat pheasant?

Can babies eat pheasant? Yes, from about 6 months, in small occasional amounts. It is lean and iron-rich, but shot game can contain lead pellets and tiny bones, so careful preparation matters.

Is pheasant safe for babies?

Occasionally, yes, if all bones and shot are removed and it is well cooked.

Is game meat good for babies?

It is lean and iron-rich, but best as an occasional food.
