What should a baby wake window app do?

A useful wake window app should use your baby’s own recent naps, night sleep and feeds rather than treat one age chart as a timetable. In OBubba, personalised timing guidance begins around day four of logging and draws on a broader sleep history across the first fortnight. Suggested times remain flexible guides: follow tired cues and current safer-sleep guidance.

Choose recent rhythm over one rigid number.

A wake window can be useful planning context, but it cannot know whether today included a short nap, a long feed, illness, travel or an unusually stimulating morning. The app should keep that context beside the next suggested time.

How to use a wake window app without chasing the clock

Start with the day that is actually happening. You do not need to rebuild yesterday or hit a perfect schedule.

Step 1

Log the nap that just happened

Add the real nap start and end, then keep the latest feed and night sleep in the same picture.

Step 2

Give the pattern a few days

During days one to three, keep logging the useful moments. Personalised timing guidance begins around day four.

Step 3

Check cues when the window approaches

Use the suggested time to look for your baby’s tired cues and adjust for the day in front of you.

Step 4

Keep sleep safety separate from timing

A timing suggestion never replaces a clear sleep space, back sleeping or individual guidance from a qualified professional.

Wake windows are planning context, not a sleep promise

  • Suggested nap and bedtime times are guides, not guarantees or medical advice.
  • Babies vary and the same baby can need a different rhythm after illness, travel, a developmental change or an unusual nap.
  • Follow your baby’s cues and current safer-sleep guidance every time.
  • If sleep, feeding, breathing, growth or wellbeing concerns you, contact an appropriate qualified professional.

No. A useful suggestion is a prompt to check the baby in front of you, not an exact deadline or guarantee.

No. Wake windows are planning context, not medical advice. Parents should follow safe sleep and professional guidance when needed.
