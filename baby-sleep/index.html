Where these numbers come from: no health body publishes official wake-window figures, so the wake-window, nap and bedtime ranges here are common practice and match the tables inside the OBubba app. Total sleep from 4 months follows the American Academy of Sleep Medicine consensus, which the American Academy of Pediatrics supports (Paruthi and colleagues, 2016). For younger babies the NHS says total sleep varies widely, from around 8 to around 18 hours a day, and that "either is perfectly normal" (NHS Best Start in Life).

Safer sleep, at every age

Whatever the age and whatever the night, The Lullaby Trust advises always putting your baby to sleep on their back in a clear cot or sleep space, in the same room as you for at least the first six months, day and night (The Lullaby Trust). The NHS has more on helping your baby to sleep.

If you are worried about your baby’s sleep, breathing, feeding or how they seem, talk to your health visitor or GP, or call NHS 111. In an emergency, call 999.
